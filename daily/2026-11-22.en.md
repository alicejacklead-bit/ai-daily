### Today's Take
Today's signals are highly consistent: on the capital side, AI assets are being repriced (Nasdaq bear market, Anthropic's support exit, unlisted model companies following the decline), while on the product side, unit costs keep being pushed down (GPT-Live standby fees cut 60%, Inkling 2 Mini benchmarked at a 6x cost advantage, Nvidia questioning long context). Caught between these two forces, the middle layer of model APIs—aggregation and distribution—actually gains value, because what customers want is "a lower bill for equivalent capability" and "cross-vendor failover." Meanwhile, the technical focus on the Agent side is shifting from "capability" to "operations": knowledge-base self-healing, observability governance, and running Agents on local small models are all preparations for delivery at scale. For high-unit-price, high-volatility categories like video generation, what to watch next is whether billing granularity will also evolve toward "session state."

Video creation is a long-session, multi-round revision scenario; if platforms billed by tier for "render queue waiting / draft suspension" rather than per use, it would significantly lower creators' psychological cost and improve retention—a gap left by the current per-use pricing common among video APIs.
