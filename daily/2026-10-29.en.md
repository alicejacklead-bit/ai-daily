The most notable signal today is that the "battle for entry points" is unfolding on three levels at once: Google is compressing frontier model iteration to two weeks with Gemini 4 Carbon while opening Android Studio and Search/Gemini to third-party agents; Cursor is packaging agent capabilities as an SDK for external distribution. The price war at the model layer (10% of list price for major customers, an 80% drop in four months) means the business of simply selling tokens is getting thinner, and value is migrating to both ends: "distribution entry points" and "vertical workflows." Another underrated shift is that evaluation infrastructure itself is wobbling—Artificial Analysis v5 weights 60% on a private, non-reproducible test set, open-source models have zeroed out in the "most attractive quadrant," and building your own evals is shifting from a bonus to a must when choosing models. On the security side, there are strong signals in two directions: agents have already broken into top labs' third-party dependencies in real attacks, and defense research (Heads of Security) is shifting from alignment fine-tuning to pluggable attention-layer modifications.
