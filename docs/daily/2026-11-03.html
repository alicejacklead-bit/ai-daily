💡 Today's Take

🎯 On OpenRouter, the API prices of a batch of flagship models have collectively risen 2–5x, while Anthropic is sprinting toward an IPO with nearly $900 billion in compute commitments and $23 billion in losses—the era of "cheap tokens" is being replaced by the era of "compute capitalization," and the distribution bargaining power of API platforms needs to be recalculated. What's most notable today isn't any single news item, but the word "cost" appearing across four directions at once: Anthropic's $900 billion compute commitment, Oracle's $20 billion debt financing, a 2–5x price hike on a batch of models on OpenRouter, and Grok Bot having its quotas cut due to unsustainable costs. This shows that the pricing logic on the AI supply side is shifting from "subsidize to grab share" to "cover real capital costs," and the low-price window that API platforms relied on for customer acquisition over the past year is narrowing. At the same time, Qwen committing to continued open source, NVIDIA adding a trustworthy foundation for Agents with a semantic layer, and LangChain hosting the Agent runtime—the demand side is shifting from "calling models" to "running workflows end to end," which is precisely the value layer that low-price models cannot replace. For video generation API platforms, this means the phase of simply competing on unit price is nearing its end; what comes next is competing on exclusive models, workflow packaging, and channel lock-in.
