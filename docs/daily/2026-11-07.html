💡 Today's Take

🎯 The competitive focus of model APIs is shifting from "how cheap each token is" to "how smoothly Agents can use them"—the major shelf reshuffling at fal and OpenRouter, and Perplexity selling a always-on Agent for $200/month, are both chasing the same group of people willing to pay for "getting tasks done." What stands out most today is not which model is stronger, but that "money and shelves" are being rearranged at the same time: Anthropic's IPO valuation was cut by buyers to 1.1–1.3 trillion, OpenAI only raised 11 billion this round, and Ramp data shows enterprise AI spending has flattened—patience on the capital side for "high growth" is declining. Meanwhile, the model lists at fal and OpenRouter completed a major overhaul within days, with Kling 3.0 and Veo 3.1 removed from the list and batch tiers appearing densely, indicating that the distribution layer is hedging against falling unit prices with "listing speed + tiered pricing." On the Agent side, Perplexity has set a new price anchor with a $200/month always-on Agent, while LangChain's browser tool tests show that the bottleneck to deployment still lies in engineering details. Taken together: marginal differences in model capability are shrinking, and competition is shifting to "who can get tasks running end-to-end and explain the cost structure clearly."

💰 Opportunities

  • A "batch/non-real-time" pricing tier for video generation APIs: OpenRouter densely rolled out batch tiers on the text side today, while video generation is inherently a long-running task and naturally asynchronous. Based on OpenRouter's model list adding entries such as deepseek-v4-flash-0731:batch, minimax-m3:batch, and qwen3.8-27b:batch, platforms offering video APIs can be the first to split out "standard/batch/preview" tiers, defining the pricing structure on the video side before aggregator platforms do.
  • Hybrid "compute + seat" billing and hosting for always-on Agent tasks: Perplexity Teammates sells an always-on Agent at $200/month, showing the market is willing to pay for "continuously running," but per-seat billing will diverge from actual compute consumption. Based on this item and Ramp's data showing "usage up, spending flat," one can offer small and medium teams hybrid billing based on task duration + tokens plus runtime hosting, solving the pain point of "bills spiraling out of control once the Agent runs."
