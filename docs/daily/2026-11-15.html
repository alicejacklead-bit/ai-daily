💡 Today's Take

🎯 Four flagship models on OpenRouter all cut prices at the same time, and DeepSeek has broken its full suite into freely composable APIs — the model layer is shifting from "selling models" to "selling assemblable building blocks," and the API platform price war has only just begun. The most noteworthy signal today is that "price cuts" and "unbundling" are happening at the same time: four flagship models on OpenRouter dropped in price overnight, DeepSeek split its app capabilities into freely composable APIs, and Anthropic used a default-model change to force everyone to rewrite their prompts. All three point to the same thing—competition at the model layer has shifted from "who is stronger" to "who is cheaper and easier to orchestrate." Meanwhile, Runway extended video-model capabilities into robot control and open-sourced it, suggesting that the second outlet for the video-generation track may not lie in creative tools. For platforms building video-model APIs, the real risk is not that some model cuts its price, but that the aggregation layer compares prices faster than they can adjust their own pricing.

💰 Opportunities

  • A "prompt compatibility layer / model migration hosting" for Agent developers: Anthropic set Opus 5.5 as the default and publicly announced that old prompt habits are slowing the model down; combined with multiple models on OpenRouter adjusting prices at the same time, teams have to rewrite prompts and rerun evals with every generation change. A hosted layer could automatically migrate prompts between old and new models, align outputs, and provide cost comparisons—based on today's Anthropic prompt guide and OpenRouter's price changes.
  • Packaging video-model capabilities for an "embodied/robotics" outlet: Runway's open-sourcing of Praxis-1 proves that knowledge from video training can transfer to robot control, but it still cannot autonomously plan complex action sequences. A combined "video model + action planning" API could be offered to robotics teams, packaging the video-generation platform's multi-turn editing and conditional-control capabilities into robot training data and controller interfaces—based on Praxis-1's top ranking on RoboLab-120 and its planning-capability gap.
