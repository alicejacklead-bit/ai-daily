💡 Today's Take

🎯 Jensen Huang's resignation, compounded by Democrats launching a congressional investigation, has turned Nvidia from the "anchor of the AI narrative" into a "source of uncertainty"—and this is exactly the window for video model API platforms to reshuffle suppliers and capture developers' mindshare. Today's signals point to the same thing: both compute and distribution are loosening at the same time. The leadership vacuum at Nvidia turns both GPU supply and the Hugging Face acquisition into variables, while the price sheets from OpenRouter and Volcano Ark show on the same day that subsidies for low-cost inference are receding, and that the video and image side is beginning to re-tier with "per-token + minimum spend" and "open-source low-cost models." For video generation API platforms, the real competitive point is shifting from "whether you can call the strongest model" to "whether you can orchestrate expensive and cheap models into the same workflow," and the free output of the Gemini Decisions API lowers the cost structure of this route by another notch. It is reasonable to expect a wave of hybrid products in the coming weeks that "use decision models for judgment and video models for generation."

The opportunity lies in packaging open-source models into a high-priced tier with consistent editing, commercial licensing, and enterprise SLAs, serving brands and e-commerce teams that cannot directly use open-source weights but have budgets—based on these two items: the MiniMax open-source image generation model and the downward shift in Seedream 5.5 pricing tiers.
